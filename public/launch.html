01 — The Problem

A concert brings thousands of people together.
Then it just ends.

You stand shoulder to shoulder with strangers, sing the same words, share the same drop — and then the lights come up. By Monday, three things have quietly died:

LOSS · 1

Your clips die scattered

The videos fans filmed get posted to social media, stripped of all context, and buried by the algorithm within hours. The night becomes noise.

LOSS · 2

People become strangers again

The person who screamed next to you at minute 47 — who shared the best moment of your month — is someone you will never find again.

LOSS · 3

Official footage goes unwatched

Organizers record the whole show professionally. Those hours of video sit on a hard drive forever, seen by no one.

The event ends — but the relationships it could have created never even start.
02 — The Insight
Shared experience is the best icebreaker.
We just give it a room.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE — RANDOM ACCESS ONE

People don't come back for the archive. They come back to relive the night — and they stay because of who they met there. Nostalgia is the hook; friendship is the goal.

03 — The Solution

A community per night.
You had to be there.

Structurally it works like Reddit: the platform hosts many communities, but each one is a single event, created by the organizer. Inside, the night is rebuilt as a timeline — and the crowd fills it with every angle of itself.

1

The timeline is the feed

The organizer uploads the official footage they recorded anyway. The night is arranged into Moments — the songs, the speeches, the drop — scrubbable and in order. You don't post into an endless feed; you post at minute 47, when the drop hit.

2

Fans add their moments

Attendees upload their own photos and videos, and each one lands at its true moment in the show. The result is the night seen from everywhere at once — the soundboard, the pit, the rail, and your spot in the crowd, pinned where it belongs.

3

Presence is the price of admission

You can only join if you were actually there — your video is your ticket stub. Everyone inside shared the same night, so the community never starts cold and never fills with tourists. Consent is captured per clip, at upload. Nothing is ever scraped.

4

Conversation grows around moments

"Wasn't that song insane?" — anchored to the exact moment it happened, with everyone's angles clustered right there. Didn't film anything? Pin a text memory instead: "this is where I lost my voice." Filming is rewarded; presence is never punished.

5

Relationships outlive the event

You meet someone in the comments who was standing ten meters away. You follow them. You see the next shows they're attending. You went in with a ticket and came out with a friend who loves the same music you do.

04 — Design Principles

Five rules the product never breaks.

THE EVENT IS THE COMMUNITY

Not a global feed — a room per night.

YOU HAD TO BE THERE

Presence-gated, always. No tourists.

CONSENT-FIRST

Every clip uploaded by its owner, with explicit permission. Never scraped.

THE TIMELINE IS THE TRUTH

Content lives at the moment it happened.

NOSTALGIA IS THE HOOK

Friendship is the goal. People return to relive; they stay for who they met.

ONE SHARED NIGHT

No personalized algorithm — the community collectively decides what sits closest to each moment.
